Think in leverage

$45 value

Most people use AI like a search engine: ask a question, get an answer, move on. The people getting real results use it like a talented employee — briefed properly, given real work, and reviewed like a manager would.

The oracle trap vs. the delegation mindset

Ask AI "what should I post?" and you get generic mush — that's the oracle trap: treating AI as a magic answer machine. Instead, delegate: hand over a defined task, with context, standards and an example of what "good" looks like. The quality of what you get back is a mirror of the brief you gave.

The 80/20 of AI in one sentence

80% of AI's value comes from 20% of use cases: drafting, repurposing, summarizing, researching, and critiquing your work. Master those five before chasing anything shinier.

Exercise: the delegation audit (10 minutes)

Write down everything you did for your business or content last week. Then mark each task:

  • A — AI does it, I review. First drafts, captions, outlines, research summaries, repurposing.
  • B — AI assists, I drive. Strategy, offers, anything with your personal story or taste.
  • C — Only me. Relationships, appearing on camera, final judgement calls.

Most people discover 40–60% of their week is sitting in column A. That's your recovered time — and everything in the next five modules is about spending it well.

Rule to keep

AI produces the first 80%. You produce the final 20% — the taste, the stories, the opinions only you have. Never ship the raw output; that final pass is where your brand lives.

The C.R.A.F.T. prompt framework

$95 value

Every high-performing prompt in this booklet is built from five parts. Learn the formula once and you can write prompts for anything — no cheat sheet needed.

CContextWhat's the situation, who's the audience, what do they care about?"I run a fitness page for busy professionals over 30…"
RRoleWho should the AI act as? A role loads expertise and standards."Act as a direct-response copywriter who writes conversational hooks…"
AActionThe specific task, with numbers. Vague asks get vague answers."Write 10 hooks under 12 words each for this topic…"
FFormatExactly how the output should be structured."Numbered list. For each: the hook + one line on why it works."
TToneThe voice. Paste a sample of your writing for best results."Confident, warm, zero corporate jargon. Match this sample: […]"
Before — the oracle trap

"Give me some Instagram post ideas about fitness."

Result: the same 10 generic ideas everyone else gets.

After — C.R.A.F.T. applied

"I coach desk workers over 30 who have 30 min/day [C]. Act as a fitness content strategist [R]. Give me 10 reel ideas that challenge a common myth [A], as a table: myth → truth → hook line [F]. Tone: blunt but encouraging [T]."

The follow-up multiplier

The first output is never the final output. Push back like you would with a junior teammate: "Make #3 punchier." · "Rewrite in my voice — sample attached." · "Now critique your own answer and fix the weakest part." Two follow-ups typically double the quality.

P2The hook machinereplaces a $150 copywriter brief

Act as a short-form copywriter. Topic: [topic]. Audience: [audience]. Write 15 scroll-stopping hooks under 12 words: 5 curiosity-based, 5 contrarian, 5 outcome-based. Then mark your top 3 and explain in one line each why they'll stop the scroll.

P3The carousel scripterreplaces a $100 design brief

P4The repurposing enginereplaces a $250/mo content assistant

Here is one piece of long-form content: [paste video script, newsletter or blog post]. Repurpose it into: 3 reel scripts (30–45s, spoken-word style with hook + payoff), 1 carousel outline, 5 tweet-length posts, and 1 email to my list. Keep my exact phrases where they're strong; flag anything that needs a personal story from me with [STORY].

The prompt vault, continued

P6The market X-rayreplaces $300 of market research

Act as a market researcher for [niche]. Map: the 5 biggest problems my audience says they have (in their own words), the 5 problems they actually have underneath, objections that stop them buying, and 3 underserved sub-niches. For each sub-niche: one content angle and one offer idea. Be specific.

P7The offer sharpenerreplaces a $200 positioning consult

Here's my offer: [describe offer + price]. Audience: [audience]. Rewrite the positioning 3 ways: (1) outcome-first, (2) enemy-first (against the old way), (3) speed/ease-first. For each: a one-sentence pitch, a 50-word description, and the strongest objection with a one-line rebuttal.

P8The 5-email welcome sequencereplaces a $350 email copy package

Write a 5-email welcome sequence for people who downloaded [your freebie]. Email 1: deliver + quick win. 2: my story — insert [STORY] markers where I should add personal detail. 3: the big mistake my audience makes. 4: case study or method walkthrough. 5: soft pitch for [offer]. Subject lines under 6 words; emails under 200 words.

P9The talking-head scriptreplaces a $150 scriptwriter

Write a 60-second talking-head video script about [topic] for [audience]. Structure: hook (first 3 seconds, no intro, no "hey guys"), stakes (why this matters now), 3 rapid points, payoff, and a CTA to [action]. Write it as spoken — short sentences, contractions. Add [PAUSE] and [B-ROLL: …] markers.

P10The brutal editorreplaces a $100 content audit

Act as a brutally honest editor who optimizes for attention and clarity. Here's my draft: [paste draft]. Score it /10 on hook, flow, specificity, and CTA. List the 3 weakest lines and rewrite each. Cut 20% of the words without losing meaning. Then show the final improved version.

Make the vault yours: these ten are templates, not scripture. Each time a prompt works, save your filled-in version to a note called "Prompt Vault" — within a month you'll own a library tuned to your niche.
